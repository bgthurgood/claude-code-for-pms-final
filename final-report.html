Rook Dispatch · Final report · Claude Code for PMs

Release 4.2 didn't make responders say no.
It made four of them disappear.

Six modules, one question: what did 4.2 actually do? This is the answer, the evidence behind it, the prompts that found it, the fix, and the check that stops the next one.

Dispatch PM · 7 Nov 2026 · Fictional course scenario. Cover names and routing status only (Security Policy 4.1).

What happened, plainly

The 60-second ping wait was just shorter than four people's answer time, and the score had no way back.

4.2 cut the ping wait from 90s to 60s and moved routing weight toward distance. Four responders (Farlight, The Undertow, Vesper, Meteor Mite) usually answer in about 61–64 seconds. They started missing pings. The code charges a miss exactly like a "no", only a taken ping adds points back, and nothing else ever restores a score. Their scores slid to zero, so routing put them last on every callout and their phones went quiet.

It wasn't seasonal, and they weren't turning jobs down. It was misses with no way back.

What the fix doesn't claim

  • Going back to 90s alone doesn't bring them back: their scores are already zero (Module 4 simulation).
  • The re-score keeps every real ping. It can't show the extra pings a higher score would have earned, or misses a lock-screen answer might have saved.
  • The rule values are placeholders. Wen sets how fast scores ease back; Ravi tests it on the ping history.
  • Today's phone steps are assumed, not yet checked with Sofia.
  • Helen decides who the reset covers and whether to say the cause was on our side.
